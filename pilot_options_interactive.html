Process Flow
Step 1
System-Wide Announcement
  • Single email to all ~400 SF employees
  • EC Benefits is open — come explore and share feedback
  • Includes access link to QA environment
  • No task assigned, no scheduling required
→
Step 2
Self-Directed Access
  • Employees access EC Benefits in QA at their own discretion
  • Explore any area freely — benefits summary, enrollment, life events
  • No scenario assigned, no completion window required
→
Step 3
Voluntary Feedback
  • Survey available at end of session (in EC Benefits if possible, or via link)
  • No required completion — fully opt-in
  • Feedback path: survey → Jira ticket or Aha enhancement card
→
Step 4
Ongoing Nudges
  • 2–3 reminder emails over the pilot window
  • Optional: feature spotlight emails to encourage testing specific areas
  • Monthly open feedback session (opt-in)
→
Step 5
Feedback Synthesis
  • Product reviews survey responses passively throughout pilot window
  • Themes surfaced to team — no individual tracking
  • End-of-pilot summary to leadership

Participant Identification

  • No identification needed — all ~400 SF employees are eligible
  • Participation is entirely self-selected
  • No event trigger, no individual outreach
  • SAP HR sends the announcement; no follow-up targeting

Environment & Scenarios

  • Environment: QA (no real qualifying events — employees explore freely)
  • No scenarios assigned — employees decide what to test
  • Risk: priority scenarios may never be covered
  • Carrier file validation not applicable (no real elections)
  • Time-elapsed testing not applicable (no structured scenario sequencing)

Communication Plan

  • Single system-wide launch announcement
  • 2–3 reminder emails distributed over the pilot window
  • Optional: area-specific spotlight emails ("Have you tried enrolling in FSA?")
  • Monthly open feedback session (opt-in, low attendance expected)
  • Lowest communication effort of all four options

Resource Requirements

  • No dedicated Project Manager required
  • SAP HR: sends announcement and reminder emails
  • Product: monitors feedback passively, hosts optional feedback sessions
  • QA environment must support broader access (~400 potential users)
  • Least overhead of all four options — but least control

Testing Schedule

  • Wave 1 — Jan 13–31: System-wide announcement; QA environment opens; employees begin self-directed exploration
  • Wave 2 — Feb 10–28: Reminder nudge; feature spotlight email (e.g., "Have you tried FSA enrollment?"); optional open feedback session
  • Open Window — Mar 1–Apr 30: Continued access; 1–2 additional reminder nudges; final call email mid-April; feedback synthesis

Open Questions

  • Is the QA environment approved and stable enough to support up to ~400 self-directed users?
  • How do we drive meaningful engagement — without a specific task, most employees won't prioritize it
  • What minimum ask do we make of employees? (e.g., "Spend 15 minutes and answer 5 questions")
  • What areas or scenarios should employees be guided toward to maximize useful feedback?
  • How do we ensure employees understand this is a test environment — not their real benefits?
  • Will employees self-exclude, assuming "I don't have a benefit change right now so this doesn't apply to me"?
  • Would this test population need to continue testing post-H1 2027 — all participants, a subset, or an additional ~6-week window before GA?

Option Comparison

All four options evaluated across dimensions most relevant to pilot success.

Option 1
Free For All
Choose this if…
The goal is maximum employee awareness with minimal coordination — or as a complement to another option to broaden reach. Best suited when volume of impressions matters more than scenario control.
Option 2
Coffee Drip
Choose this if…
Team capacity is limited and a lightweight, low-overhead proof-of-concept is the goal — or if Project Manager and QA environment approvals cannot be secured before January.
Option 3 — Recommended
Visibility Path
Choose this if…
Maximum quality feedback, controlled test scenarios, and executive visibility into EC Benefits are the priority — and a dedicated Project Manager can be identified before December.
Option 4
Hybrid
Choose this if…
Both a dedicated Project Manager and QA environment access are confirmed — and the team wants the highest possible validation coverage and scenario breadth before GA.

Product Team Recommendation — Option 3 (Visibility Path)

Option 3 delivers the highest quality feedback in the shortest time, gives the team full control over test scenarios, and creates direct executive visibility into EC Benefits before the August 2027 GA. The key dependencies — a dedicated Project Manager and QA environment approval — are manageable and worth pursuing. Option 4 is the ceiling if Project Manager capacity allows. Option 1 is a low-overhead complement worth layering on top of any other option to drive broader awareness, but should not stand alone if meaningful scenario coverage and carrier file testing are required. Option 2 is the fallback if all other dependencies cannot be resolved before January.
